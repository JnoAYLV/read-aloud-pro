# Aviation and air cargo ground operations (built-in guide)

General industry knowledge. Company and customer definitions in the uploaded library take priority.
If a term in the sources is not here or in the library, keep it as written and do not guess.

## Locations and timing
- **Gateway**: an air cargo hub or station where aircraft are loaded and unloaded. Often named by airport code.
  ICAO codes have four letters (KMSP = Minneapolis-St Paul); IATA codes have three (MSP).
- **Block out / block in**: aircraft leaves / arrives at the gate (chocks off / on). **Wheels up / down**: takeoff / landing.
- **Turn** (turnaround): the ground time between block in and block out, including unload, service and load.
- **OTP**: on-time performance. **D0**: departing at or before scheduled time (zero minutes late). D15 = within 15 minutes.
- **Cut-off**: the latest time freight, documents or the load plan must be ready for a flight.
- **Wave**: a batch of flights or sorts handled together.

## Cargo handling
- **ULD** (unit load device): a container or pallet loaded onto the aircraft. Containers (e.g. LD3, AKE) and pallets (e.g. PMC, PAG) have type codes.
- **Build-up**: loading freight onto ULDs for departure. **Breakdown**: unloading ULDs from arrivals.
- **Contour**: the shape a built ULD must fit for its aircraft position. Over-contour freight cannot be loaded.
- **Missort**: a package or ULD sent to the wrong destination or position. **Misroute**: sent on the wrong flight or route.
- **AWB** (air waybill): the shipping document. **MAWB / HAWB**: master and house air waybills. **Manifest**: list of what is on a flight.
- **Offload**: freight removed from a flight it was planned on. **Short-shipped**: freight that missed its flight.

## Load control and weight & balance
- **W&B** (weight and balance): making sure the aircraft's total weight and centre of gravity are within limits.
- **Load plan / LIR** (loading instruction report): which ULD goes in which aircraft position.
- **Load sheet**: the final weight and balance document signed before departure.
- Loading not matching the plan is a serious safety issue: it can put the centre of gravity out of limits.
- **ULD weight discrepancy**: actual weight differs from the declared weight; must be corrected before departure.

## Dangerous goods
- **DG / hazmat**: regulated under IATA Dangerous Goods Regulations and, in the US, 49 CFR.
- **NOTOC** (notification to captain): tells the flight crew what dangerous goods are on board and where.
- Lithium batteries, dry ice and flammables are common in e-commerce freight. Damaged or leaking DG is reported immediately.

## Ramp and aircraft safety
- **GSE** (ground support equipment): tugs, dollies, K-loaders / main deck loaders, belt loaders, tow tractors, GPUs.
- **FOD** (foreign object debris): loose items that can be ingested by engines or damage tyres. FOD walks prevent it.
- **Wing walker / marshaller**: guides aircraft and equipment movement near the aircraft.
- **Chocks and cones**: chocks stop wheels moving; cones mark the aircraft's safety perimeter.
- **Aircraft damage (AC damage)**: any contact between equipment and an aircraft must be reported immediately,
  even if no damage is visible. Hidden damage can be dangerous in flight.
- **Jet blast / engine ingestion zones**: no-go areas when engines run.
- Common injury risks: lifting and ergonomics, pinch points, slips and trips, struck-by equipment, strap and net handling.

## Aircraft status
- **AOG** (aircraft on ground): an aircraft that cannot fly until fixed; urgent parts and support.
- **MEL** (minimum equipment list): items that may be inoperative for a flight under set conditions.
- **ACMI**: an operator provides aircraft, crew, maintenance and insurance for another company.

## Regulators and programmes
- **FAA**: US civil aviation regulator. **TSA**: US security, including air cargo screening. **CCSF**: certified cargo screening facility.
- **OSHA**: US workplace safety regulator.
- **IATA**: airline industry body; publishes cargo, ULD and DG standards.

## Writing about ground operations
- State the flight, gateway, time (with time zone) and position when describing an event.
- Treat any weight & balance, DG or aircraft damage issue as a safety issue, ahead of on-time or cost.
- Describe delays with the cause category (controllable / uncontrollable) and minutes of delay.
