# Email writing skills (built-in guide)

Use this guide whenever you draft an email or reply.

## 1. Bottom line first
- The first sentence says what the reader needs to know or do. Context comes after.
- If there is an ask, it is in the first two lines, with an owner and a date.
- A reader who stops after the first paragraph must still have the point.

## 2. Subject lines
- Specific and scannable: what, where, when. Example: "KMSP 7 Nov: 22-min late departure, staffing root cause".
- Add a tag when it helps triage: [Action], [FYI], [Decision needed], [Safety].
- Update the subject when the topic of a thread changes.

## 3. Structure
- Short paragraphs (1 to 3 sentences). Bullets for lists of facts, issues or actions.
- One topic per email. If there are two unrelated asks, send two emails.
- Close with clear next steps: who does what by when.
- Typical order: Bottom line → What happened (facts) → Impact → What we are doing → What I need from you.

## 4. Facts and numbers
- Use only facts that are in the sources. Never invent numbers, names, times or dates.
- Write [brackets] where something must be filled in: [time], [name], [number of AWBs].
- Give numbers with their unit and period: "14 missorts on wave 2, 7 Nov", not "lots of missorts".
- When comparing, give the baseline: "OT 6.1% vs 4.0% target".
- Separate fact from interpretation: "The departure was 22 minutes late (fact). Staffing appears to be the cause (assessment)."

## 5. Tone
- Professional, direct and calm. No exclamation marks in problem emails.
- Describe problems in terms of the process, not the person. Say "the build team was two people short", not "Bob's team failed".
- Own our part plainly: "We missed the 04:10 cut-off; this was within our control."
- Thank people for specific things, not in general.
- Match the reader: executives get the bottom line and the decision; the field gets the detail and the steps.

## 6. Common email types
**Incident or safety report.** What happened, when, where, who was affected (roles, not names unless required), immediate actions taken, current status, next steps and owner. Safety first, before operational impact.

**Escalation.** The issue in one line, impact, what has been tried, the specific decision or help needed, the deadline, and the consequence of no action.

**Status update.** On track / at risk / off track up front, then the 3 to 5 things that matter, then risks and asks.

**Reply to a complaint.** Acknowledge, state the facts we know, say what we are doing and by when, commit to a follow-up. Do not argue or speculate.

**Request.** What you need, why, by when, and what format. Make it easy to say yes.

**Recap after a meeting.** Decisions made, actions with owners and dates, open questions.

## 7. Length
- Most emails: under 150 words. Executive updates: under 200 words plus an optional detail section.
- If it needs more than a screen, write a document and send a short email with the bottom line and a link.

## 8. Before sending (checklist)
- Is the ask clear, with an owner and a date?
- Are all numbers sourced? Are blanks marked [ ]?
- Would this be fair if forwarded to the person it mentions?
- Is anything confidential or sensitive included that this audience should not see?
- Draft only: a person reviews and sends. Never state that the email has been sent.
